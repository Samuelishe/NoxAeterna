# Regression Cases

Use these as training examples for review behavior, not as universal pack requirements. Read the actual task and pack contracts for each new asset.

## 1. Count hallucination — Nine of Cups

- Contract: exactly nine cups.
- Literal result: upper group `4`, lower group `6`, total `10`.
- Lesson: count visible objects and their relevant components; never accept the prompt or prior self-review as evidence.
- Result: blocking exact-count defect; correction required when the state machine permits it.

## 2. Spatial absurdity — Queen of Swords

- Literal result: the man is outside the rope rail, with no complete supporting platform under his feet, apparently standing or hanging over a void.
- The exact one-sword contract may still pass.
- Lesson: satisfying the weapon contract does not make an unsupported human position acceptable.
- Result: blocking spatial-absurdity defect; correction required.

## 3. Role ambiguity — Hierophant

- Literal result: a large mature woman stands near two younger people, but the scene primarily reads as a demonstration or inspection of a measuring instrument.
- Lesson: size and central placement do not prove the Hierophant archetype. Institutional authority, transmission of tradition, formal initiation, two concrete initiates, and their distinction from witnesses must read without the record.
- Result: blocking story and role-legibility defect; correction required.

## 4. Diagrammatic overlay — Wheel of Fortune

- Literal result: white movement arrows appear as infographic annotation rather than diegetic elements.
- Lesson: explanatory arrows, chevrons, and overlays can destroy scene integrity even when they clarify intended motion.
- Result: blocking accidental-semantics defect.

## 5. Anachronistic instrument — Seven of Pentacles

- Literal result: a vertical device reads as a modern thermometer.
- Lesson: label-looking scales, tubes, indicators, and lab-device silhouettes must be tested against the declared technological level.
- Result: blocking historical/material defect when modern instrumentation is forbidden.

## 6. Forced motif branding

- Literal result: a recurring motif appears as a random paw stamp or doorway relief without narrative function.
- Lesson: a pack motif is not a quota. Integrate it through story, culture, material, character, or location, or omit it when ambient-only/absence is allowed.
- Result: blocking forced-motif defect when it contradicts pack art direction.

## 7. Incomplete object

- Literal result: an empty sheath is present, but no complete sword is visible.
- Lesson: a container or accessory is not the contained object; inspect blade, guard, grip, tip, and sheath separately.
- Result: exact-object contract fails.

## 8. Hidden object

- Claim: a required item is supposedly behind another object.
- Literal result: the item cannot be verified in the pixels.
- Lesson: do not count inferred, hidden, or prompt-declared objects.
- Result: treat the required object as absent unless the contract explicitly permits invisibility, which exact-count contracts normally should not.

## 9. Repeated count regression

- A correction can repair anatomy while introducing or retaining a count failure.
- Lesson: rerun every gate on every new candidate. Never inherit the previous count result.
- State-machine consequence: a blocking defect after `G2` automatically authorizes `G3`; do not stop solely because one correction was already attempted.
